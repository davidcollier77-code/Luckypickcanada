<h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="other-timeline-features">Other Timeline Features<a href="#other-timeline-features" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Other Timeline Features" title="Direct link to Other Timeline Features" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<ul>
<li class="">
<p>Speed up or slow down the entire timeline with its <code>timeScale()</code> method. You can even tween it to gradually speed up or slow down the animation smoothly!</p>
</li>
<li class="">
<p>Get or set the progress of the timeline using its <code>progress()</code> or <code>totalProgress()</code> methods (totalProgress() just includes any repeats). For example, to skip to the halfway point, set <code>myTimeline.progress(0.5);</code>.</p>

</li>
<li class="">

<h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="mechanics">How do timelines work?<a href="#mechanics" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to How do timelines work?" title="Direct link to How do timelines work?" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>Every animation (Tween and Timeline) is placed on a parent Timeline. In a sense, they all have their own playheads (that&#x27;s what its &quot;time&quot; refers to, or &quot;totalTime&quot; which is identical except that it includes repeats and repeatDelays) and when the parent&#x27;s playhead moves to a new position, it updates the childrens&#x27; too (unless they&#x27;re paused).</p>
<p>When a timeline renders at a particular time, it loops through its children and says &quot;okay, you should render as if your playhead is at ____&quot; and if that child is a Timeline with children, it does the same to its children, right on down the line. So the playheads generally remain synchronized.</p>
<p>When you unpause an animation (<code>resume()</code> or <code>play()</code>), it essentially picks up the playhead and moves it so that its internal playhead is synchronized with wherever the parent&#x27;s playhead is at that moment, thus things play perfectly smoothly. That is, unless the timeline&#x27;s <code>smoothChildTiming</code> is <code>false</code> in which case that child won&#x27;t move - its <code>startTime</code> will remain locked to where it was.</p>
<p>So basically when <code>smoothChildTiming</code> is <code>true</code>, the engine will rearrange things on the fly to ensure the playheads line up so that playback feels seamless and smooth. The same thing happens when you <code>reverse()</code> or alter the <code>timeScale</code>, etc. - the animation&#x27;s startTime shifts automatically. But sometimes you might not want that behavior - that&#x27;s when <code>smoothChildTiming: false</code> is handy on a parent timeline.</p>
<p>One more example: let&#x27;s say you&#x27;ve got a 10-second tween that&#x27;s just sitting on the root timeline and you&#x27;re 2-seconds into the tween. Let&#x27;s assume it started at exactly 0 on the root to make this easy, and then when it&#x27;s at 2-seconds, you do <code>tween.seek(5)</code>. The playhead of the root isn&#x27;t affected - it keeps going exactly as it always did, but in order to make that tween jump to 5 seconds and play appropriately, the tween&#x27;s <code>startTime</code> gets changed to -3. That way, the tween&#x27;s playhead and the root playhead are perfectly aligned.</p>
